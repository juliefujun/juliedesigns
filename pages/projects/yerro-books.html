During my internship at Yerro Books, I explored whether it was possible to implement AI into the workflow of designing book covers. I created several book covers with varying genres to test the limitations and strengths of platforms such as ChatGPT, Gemini, and Adobe Firefly.

Contents

AI Workflow

I began by developing our guiding question: How might we create a system that aids in the process of designing editorial covers using AI by conceptualizing ideas through written text while always maintaining professional standards of originality, visual quality, and legal safety regarding copyright and plagiarism?

Research

Existing research suggests AI is most effective as an ideation tool rather than a replacement for the design process. While it can quickly generate concepts, it often lacks originality, creative reasoning, and reliable attribution, raising ethical concerns around plagiarism and ownership. This informed our goal of testing where AI could responsibly fit within a designer's workflow.

Process

To evaluate AI's role in book cover design, we created four fictional books across the genres of romance, dark fantasy, science fiction, and fairytale fantasy. Each concept was developed using structured prompts that included the title, author, genre, and synopsis.

Our workflow moved through three stages: research, concept development, and image generation. To encourage ethical outputs, prompts included constraints such as avoiding named artists, copyrighted properties, and unattributed references.

While AI generated convincing visuals, many concepts closely resembled existing book covers, making originality difficult to verify. This led us to reconsider AI's role in the creative process.

Rather than asking AI to generate original concepts, we shifted to a designer led workflow. We began with hand-drawn sketches, then used AI to visualize our ideas before refining the final designs ourselves. This approach maintained creative authorship while using AI to support visualization, typography, and color exploration.

Conclusion

Our testing showed that AI is most valuable as a research and visualization tool rather than a source of original design concepts. By establishing clear ethical constraints and beginning with designer created ideas, AI became a collaborative tool that supported the design process without replacing it.
